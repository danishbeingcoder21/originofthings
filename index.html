The 20 Chronicles of Origin

Read detailed, Wikipedia-backed stories on how these essentials came into being.

Cosmology ~13.8 Billion B.C.

1. The Universe (The Big Bang)

The entire observable cosmos originated from an infinitesimally dense, hot singularity that expanded violently in cosmic inflation, creating spacetime, subatomic particles, and fundamental forces.

Wikipedia Fact: Edwin Hubble discovered in 1929 that distant galaxies are moving away from us, proving the universe is constantly expanding.
Planetary ~4.54 Billion B.C.

2. Planet Earth

Formed through accretion from the solar nebula — a swirling disc of dust and gas leftover from the Sun's creation. Heavy metals sank to create the iron core, yielding our protective magnetic field.

Wikipedia Fact: The Giant Impact Hypothesis posits that Earth collided with a Mars-sized planetoid named Theia to create our Moon.
Hydrology ~4.4 Billion B.C.

3. Liquid Water

Earth's water is believed to be older than the Sun itself. Primordial water originated from volcanic outgassing and asteroid/comet bombardment during the Late Heavy Bombardment epoch.

Wikipedia Fact: Deuterium-to-hydrogen ratios prove carbonaceous chondrite meteorites carried the majority of water to early Earth.
Biology ~3.7 Billion B.C.

4. Life (Abiogenesis)

Life sparked in deep oceanic hydrothermal vents, where mineral rich thermal gradients synthesized organic molecules like RNA, giving rise to LUCA (Last Universal Common Ancestor).

Wikipedia Fact: Stromatolites found in Western Australia represent the earliest fossil evidence of microbial mats dating back 3.48 billion years.
Paleontology ~243 Million B.C.

5. The Dinosaurs

Appearing during the Triassic period, dinosaurs dominated terrestrial ecosystems for 165 million years until the Chicxulub asteroid impact triggered the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction.

Wikipedia Fact: Modern birds are avian dinosaurs belonging to the Theropod clade, meaning dinosaurs never truly went extinct.
Anthropology ~300,000 B.C.

6. Anatomically Modern Humans

Homo sapiens emerged in the African continent, distinguished by advanced cognition, symbolic culture, complex language, and interbreeding with archaic hominins like Neanderthals.

Wikipedia Fact: The oldest discovered skeletal remains of modern Homo sapiens were unearthed in Jebel Irhoud, Morocco.
Survival Tech ~1.0 Million B.C.

7. Controlled Fire

Harnessed by Homo erectus, fire transformed human biology. Cooking food reduced digestive energy, catalyzing rapid brain growth and establishing communal campfires.

Wikipedia Fact: Microscopic traces of wood ash in South Africa's Wonderwerk Cave confirm deliberate hearth combustion 1 million years ago.
Mechanics ~4,000 B.C.

8. The Wheel

Contrary to popular belief, wheels were not invented for carts. They emerged as potter's wheels in Mesopotamia and only centuries later were combined with axled vehicles for transit.

Wikipedia Fact: The wheel is entirely anthropogenic — no mechanical wheel-and-axle exists in macro nature due to vascular tissue constraints.
Linguistics ~3,400 B.C.

9. Written Language

Originating in Sumer (modern Iraq) as Cuneiform, writing began not for literature, but as accounting ledgers to record grain, cattle, and tax debts on clay tablets.

Wikipedia Fact: The world's oldest recorded customer complaint was written on a cuneiform tablet by Nanni to merchant Ea-nasir concerning bad copper.
Economics ~600 B.C.

10. Money & Minted Coins

Barter systems faced the 'coincidence of wants' dilemma. The Kingdom of Lydia (modern Turkey) minted the first stamped coins from electrum (natural gold-silver alloy).

Wikipedia Fact: Paper fiat currency was pioneered during the Song Dynasty in 7th century China, originally as redeemable promissory notes.
Media ~105 A.D.

11. Paper Manufacture

Cai Lun, a court official during China's Han Dynasty, invented papermaking using pulverized mulberry bark, hemp waste, rags, and fishnet pulp suspended in water molds.

Wikipedia Fact: The secret of paper reached Europe via the Battle of Talas in 751 AD when Islamic armies captured Chinese papermakers.
Revolution ~1440 A.D.

12. The Printing Press

Johannes Gutenberg integrated movable metal type, oil-based inks, and wooden screw presses, igniting the European Renaissance, Protestant Reformation, and Scientific Revolution.

Wikipedia Fact: Before Gutenberg, an entire European monastery scribed only 1–2 books per year by hand using animal vellum.
Botany ~850 A.D.

13. Coffee

Discovered on the Ethiopian plateau when goatherd Kaldi observed his goats dancing with energy after chewing red berries from the Coffea arabica shrub.

Wikipedia Fact: The 17th-century European coffeehouses were named "penny universities" because a penny bought coffee and intellectual debate.
Medicine 1928 A.D.

14. Antibiotics (Penicillin)

Alexander Fleming returned to his untidy London laboratory to discover mould (Penicillium notatum) had contaminated a Staphylococcus petri dish, secreting a bacteria-killing liquid.

Wikipedia Fact: The mass production of penicillin saved an estimated 100 million lives during WWII and revolutionized modern surgery.
Physics 1831 A.D.

15. Electromagnetic Generation

While static electricity was known to Greeks through amber rubbing, Michael Faraday unveiled electromagnetic induction, moving magnets within wire coils to produce alternating electric current.

Wikipedia Fact: The "War of the Currents" between Edison's DC and Tesla/Westinghouse's AC established alternating current as the global grid power standard.
Aeronautics 1903 A.D.

16. Powered Flight

Orville and Wilbur Wright achieved the first controlled, sustained flight in a gasoline-engine heavier-than-air biplane at Kitty Hawk, North Carolina, staying aloft for 12 seconds.

Wikipedia Fact: Their key breakthrough was 3-axis control (wing warping, rudder, elevator), solving equilibrium rather than just lift.
Computing 1936 A.D.

17. Programmable Computers

Rooted in Alan Turing's theoretical Universal Machine and Babbage's mechanical Analytical Engine, computing leaped forward with the 1947 invention of the silicon semiconductor transistor.

Wikipedia Fact: ENIAC (1945), one of the first electronic general-purpose computers, contained 18,000 vacuum tubes and weighed 30 metric tons.
Cyber 1969 A.D.

18. The Internet

Funded by the US Department of Defense (DARPA), ARPANET sent the first packet-switched message ("LO") between UCLA and Stanford computers, establishing the groundwork for TCP/IP.

Wikipedia Fact: The message was meant to be "LOGIN", but the system crashed right after transmitting the letters "L" and "O".
Software 1989 A.D.

19. The World Wide Web

Sir Tim Berners-Lee at CERN proposed a global hypertext system to link research papers via HTTP, HTML, and URLs, liberating information globally and donating the code royalty-free.

Wikipedia Fact: The very first webpage is still viewable at `info.cern.ch`, hosted on Berners-Lee's NeXT workstation.
Cognition 1956 A.D.

20. Artificial Intelligence

Formally born at the Dartmouth Summer Research Workshop, AI transitioned from symbolic logic trees to deep artificial neural networks capable of natural language, pattern inference, and reasoning.

Wikipedia Fact: The phrase "Artificial Intelligence" was coined in 1955 by computer scientist John McCarthy in a conference proposal.
